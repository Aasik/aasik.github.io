Learning and Reflections

Notes on what I have learned from teaching, research, industry work and volunteering. I add to this page as I go.

What teaching has taught me

Teaching programming to beginners has shown me that students learn fastest when they run code, break it and fix it. I now build every session around short practical tasks, debugging exercises and small projects, rather than long explanations alone.

Explaining the same idea in several ways, such as a flowchart, pseudocode and Python, helps students see what stays the same behind the syntax.

Moving between industry and academia

Working in infrastructure and technical support before teaching gave me a practical view of how systems behave in real environments. I bring that into class when I talk about debugging, security and good engineering habits.

Learning through research

My MSc research on explainable deep learning for plant disease identification has taught me that a model's accuracy is only part of the story. People also need to understand why it made a decision before they trust it. That idea connects directly to my wider interest in Explainable AI and cybersecurity.

Learning by writing and sharing

As an editor at freeCodeCamp, I am learning how to explain technical topics clearly for a global audience. I plan to publish articles regularly on programming fundamentals, AI and ML, cybersecurity and cloud computing, and teaching.

What I am learning next

Explainable AI methods such as Grad-CAM, LIME and SHAP, cloud security and incident response, and better ways to teach programming and AI with hands-on practice.
